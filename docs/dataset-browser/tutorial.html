How to use Data Hunter

Ten steps from a new research question to a registered, licence-reviewed dataset selection: create the study, search for its datasets, then explore, shortlist, review and acquire. Each step links to the page where it happens.

How the pieces fit together

Everything on the left happens in your browser and changes nothing outside it. Each hand-off in the middle is a prompt, CSV or JSON you give to Claude Code or Codex, which works under the reviewed agent definitions and writes only to the registries and holdings on the right. This site is then rebuilt from those records.

Boxes are the pages and files named in the steps below. Arrows carry data, not permissions: no step grants data or AI-use rights.

  1. 1

    Create a research study

    Open Research studies and fill in the new-study form: a stable ID, the research question, intended use, population, targets and inclusion/exclusion rules. These words drive the dataset search in the next step, so be specific.

    Saving the study makes it the active one everywhere: the Study picker in the header switches between studies, and shortlists, annotations, saved filter views and the action log belong to the active study. Dataset files are shared holdings.

    Permissions are never inherited: a dataset approved for one study still needs review for another. An existing study can be imported from its exported JSON instead.

    Research studies
  2. 2

    Search for datasets for the study

    After saving, the site opens Add datasets in discovery mode with the study brief filled in. Review the generated prompt and pass it to Claude Code or Codex with the intake-agent definition.

    The agent searches from scratch for this study, retains public licence and access notices with URL, date and hash within a small metadata budget, and only then matches results to existing catalogue IDs. It is licence-first and review-only: no dataset payload is transferred.

    Results come back as study annotations (relevance, role, tags), a suggested shortlist and a discovery report registered with the study. New sources appear as catalogue entries with their licence evidence attached.

    Add datasetsResearch studies
  3. 3

    Explore the catalogue

    Open the Dataset explorer; it opens on the study’s shortlist when one exists. Search by name, ID, study, topic or your own tags; tick filter options in the left panel. Ticks within a group match any option; groups combine.

    Under This research study, filter by the relevance, role, tags and shortlist the search produced. Under Development readiness, “Needs preparation / permission review” and “Incomplete” have nested reasons that say why entries are not ready. Every option has an ⓘ explanation and a live count.

    Drag the handle between the filters and the table to widen the panel; the width is remembered in this browser. Sort by priority, name or retained size. Copy link shares the exact view.

  4. 4

    Read a dataset record

    Open a row to see what the resource is good for, which files are held and verified, input/output/reference formats, and the published studies that used it.

    Read Problems & next steps for the recorded blockers (the catalogue row links there as “What’s missing”) and Access & licensing for the access route, terms and the local AI permission status for the active study.

    “Back to your selection” returns to the catalogue with your filters and ticks intact.

    Dataset explorer
  5. 5

    Refine the study shortlist

    Tick datasets in the table (or Tick all matching). Ticks survive searching and filtering; the selection bar says how many ticked datasets the current filters hide.

    Save to study shortlist adds the ticked datasets to the active study’s shortlist. With nothing ticked it offers to replace the shortlist with everything matching, after confirmation.

    Filter by Study shortlist to see it, and Export CSV for a metadata-only table of the ticked or matching entries. Nothing is downloaded.

    Dataset explorer
  6. 6

    Review the annotations

    The dataset search (step 2) annotates every candidate for the study automatically: relevance, role (development, benchmark, reference, exclude), tags and a suggested shortlist, registered with the study and shown on the catalogue filters and dataset pages.

    You only step in where you disagree. On a dataset page, change the relevance, role, tags or note; on Published studies & tools, filter papers by annotation, reported task and the permission status of their linked datasets, and adjust paper annotations the same way.

    Your own changes live in this browser until you export and register the study (step 10); registered annotations from the search stay visible alongside them.

    Published studies & tools
  7. 7

    Record a licence decision

    When you have read the exact licence or agreement, open the dataset page’s Review licence and record decision form. Cite the retained notice, its SHA-256 and the official URL, and tick only the operations and files you actually reviewed.

    Generate the decision, then hand the prompt or JSON to the agent, which validates and registers it with Scripts/review_dataset_license.py.

    A manual decision cannot override an explicit prohibition, supply a missing agreement, or turn AI-silent terms into explicit approval. Saving the form grants nothing by itself.

    Licensing
  8. 8

    Prepare a download

    Tick the datasets, then Download…. Choose the scope: investigate and register only, the reviewed minimal package, or a specified full release. Choose lenient or strict licensing and, if needed, byte limits and technical exceptions.

    Generate the prompt or save the task CSV and give it to Claude Code or Codex with the download-agent definition. The agent resolves exact versions and manifests, checks rights and access, and never labels partial holdings complete.

    Downloading grants no AI-use permission: the source-bound preflight runs again before any inference or embeddings.

  9. 9

    Work with held files

    For ticked datasets with retained files, More actions offers verify, copy, prepare workflow inputs, plan a move and plan a removal, plus a file-inventory export.

    Each action produces a CSV task for the operations agent. Removal and relocation are plans only; the agent asks for confirmation of the concrete file list before changing anything.

    Dataset explorer
  10. 10

    Export and register the study

    On Research studies, Export study, shortlist & annotations saves a JSON file. Pass it to Claude Code or Codex to register with Scripts/register_research_study.py --request /path/to/study.json --apply.

    Registration makes the brief, shortlist and annotations durable and appends this browser’s actions to the study’s log. Registered reports and the log appear on the same page. You can register early and re-register after each round of changes.

    Registration grants no data permissions either; it records what was decided and done. To drop a study later: a browser draft is deleted on the Research studies page; a registered one is removed with Scripts/register_research_study.py --remove STUDY_ID --apply, which keeps its reports and log.

What this site never does

The home page lists what the site never does: no network requests or model calls, no permissions granted, no downloads and no clinical certification. <a href="index.html#how-it-works">Read it there</a>.
